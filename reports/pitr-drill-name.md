| Recovery time (RTO): damaged primary stopped until the restored one accepts writes | 63.90 s |

The heartbeat client wrote one row every 0.1 s throughout; its log is the evidence for the acknowledged-writes checks. A write counts as committed before the restore point when the statement that recorded the target could see it, and as started after it when the WAL position read inside its own transaction is past the position recorded at the target, so neither check compares the client's clock with the server's.

Restoring in place goes back in time for the whole database: the writes the application made after the accident are lost with it. Restoring a copy to a side instance and moving the deleted rows back instead keeps them (docs/pitr.md).
